**1.	Title**

**Intelligent and Secure Communications in Non-Terrestrial Networks**


**2.	Background, Motivation, and Technical Scope of the Proposed Special Issue**

The evolution toward 6G-enabled global connectivity is driving an unprecedented integration of non-terrestrial networks (NTNs) into future wireless infrastructures. While NTNs are essential for ubiquitous coverage, emergency response, and seamless connectivity, their broadcast nature, long propagation distances, highly dynamic topologies, and limited onboard resources introduce fundamentally new security, privacy, and reliability challenges that are not adequately addressed by existing terrestrial communication paradigms.

At the same time, emerging applications such as tactical IoT, emergency communications, intelligent transportation, and military–civil fusion systems require not only conventional cryptographic security, but also low-probability-of-detection (LPD), low-probability-of-intercept (LPI), and covert communication capabilities, especially in contested or adversarial environments. These requirements call for cross-layer, intelligence-driven, and physics-aware communication designs that go beyond traditional security mechanisms.

Recent advances in artificial intelligence, learning-based signal processing, semantic communications, and reconfigurable radio technologies offer powerful new tools to enable intelligent, adaptive, and proactive secure/covert communication strategies in NTNs. However, the integration of intelligence with physical-layer security, covert communication theory, and NTN-specific channel characteristics remains an open and timely research challenge. Issues such as AI-assisted attack detection, learning-based beamforming for covertness, secured integrated sensing and communication design, and resilience against intelligent adversaries are still in their infancy and lack a dedicated forum.

The main GLOBECOM symposia primarily focus on general wireless communications, networking, and security techniques, with limited emphasis on the unique interplay among intelligence, security, covertness, and non-terrestrial propagation environments. In contrast, this workshop aims to provide a focused and interdisciplinary platform that brings together researchers and practitioners from NTN communications, physical-layer security, covert communications, and AI-enabled wireless systems, fostering discussions on emerging threats, novel theoretical frameworks, and practical system designs tailored specifically for 6G non-terrestrial and integrated ground–air–space networks.

• By addressing intelligent, secure, and covert communication paradigms in NTNs—topics not explicitly covered by existing GLOBECOM workshops or symposia—the proposed workshop is highly timely and directly aligned with the vision of resilient, trustworthy, and intelligent 6G global connectivity.

**6.	Publicity and Promotion Plan**


The proposed workshop will be actively publicized through multiple coordinated channels to ensure broad visibility and to attract high-quality submissions and participation from both academia and industry.


First, the Call for Papers (CFP) will be disseminated through well-established IEEE ComSoc mailing lists, including relevant Technical Committees (TCs) such as Satellite and Space Communications, Communication Theory, Signal Processing for Communications, and Wireless Communications. This will ensure targeted outreach to researchers whose expertise aligns closely with the workshop scope.
Second, the workshop will be promoted via professional academic networks, including the personal and institutional mailing lists of the workshop organizers and Technical Program Committee members. Special emphasis will be placed on engaging early-career researchers and doctoral students, as well as researchers working at the intersection of 6G, NTNs, security, covert communications, and AI-enabled wireless systems.


Third, the CFP and key updates (submission deadline, notification of acceptance, program highlights) will be shared through professional social media platforms (e.g., LinkedIn, X/Twitter, ResearchGate) and relevant research community forums, following IEEE and ComSoc communication guidelines. These efforts will help reach a broader and more diverse international audience.
In addition, the organizers will coordinate with related workshops and special sessions at IEEE conferences (e.g., GLOBECOM, ICC, WCNC) to cross-promote the event and encourage submissions on emerging and interdisciplinary topics. Invitations will also be extended to distinguished researchers and industry experts for keynote and panel participation, further enhancing the workshop’s visibility and attractiveness.


Through this multi-channel and community-driven promotion strategy, the workshop aims to ensure strong participation, high-quality submissions, and a well-balanced technical program, contributing to the overall success of IEEE GLOBECOM 2026.

**IEEE Open Journal of the Communications Society Call for Papers** 


**Intelligent Covert Communications for Next-Generation Wireless Networks**


As 6G networks move toward ubiquitous connectivity, extreme reliability, and pervasive intelligence, ensuring privacy and trustworthiness becomes a fundamental design goal. With the proliferation of sensing devices, artificial intelligence (AI)-driven monitoring, and open-access communication environments, users and systems face unprecedented exposure to data leakage and traffic analysis. Traditional security mechanisms that focus solely on encryption and authentication are no longer sufficient to protect communication activities from detection or inference. To safeguard critical applications such as autonomous systems and confidential Internet of Things (IoT) operations, the ability to transmit without being detected has become an essential requirement for next-generation networks.


Covert communication addresses this challenge by concealing the very existence of information transmission, making it statistically indistinguishable from environmental noise. This unique property enables stealthy and privacy-preserving connectivity in adversarial environments. However, as wireless systems become more complex and dynamic, conventional covert communication methods that rely on static models or fixed thresholds are increasingly ineffective. This motivates the rise of intelligent covert communication, where AI, digital twin (DT), and integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) technologies are leveraged to create adaptive, context-aware, and self-optimizing covert systems. Intelligent covert communication enables transmitters and receivers to learn environmental dynamics, predict detection risks, and autonomously adjust strategies to achieve optimal covertness and reliability.


This Special Issue aims to bring together cutting-edge research that advances the theory, design, and application of intelligent covert communication for next-generation wireless networks. It seeks to explore how intelligence can empower covert systems to achieve secure, stealthy, and resilient connectivity across terrestrial, non-terrestrial, and hybrid communication infrastructures.
