## Scope

Low-altitude wireless networks (LAWNs) support autonomous aerial operations in environments characterized by near-ground blockage and rapidly changing propagation conditions. The interaction between flight trajectories, wireless link visibility, sensing geometry, and networked observations creates tightly coupled relationships between communication systems and aerial platform dynamics.

Distributed collaborative intelligence in LAWNs must operate under partial observations and intermittent connectivity. Estimation errors, delayed information updates, and shared radio resources introduce additional challenges for cooperative control and real-time inference. These challenges motivate the joint design of physical-layer signal processing and networked decision-making to improve the performance of collective aerial tasks.

This special issue focuses on advances in signal processing for cyber-physical LAWNs, particularly multifunctional signal processing, cooperative inference, and intelligent swarm control. Contributions are expected to establish explicit connections between wireless communication constraints and physical system dynamics, supported by theoretical analysis, algorithmic development, or experimental validation under realistic low-altitude conditions.

### Topics of Interest

- Motion-aware channel estimation for low-altitude wireless links
- Multifunctional signal design for joint communication and sensing
- Cooperative localization in GNSS-challenged environments
- Distributed perception over bandwidth-constrained aerial links
- Communication–control co-design for aerial swarms
- Network topology design considering swarm coordination
- Decentralized learning under intermittent connectivity
- Aerial–edge collaboration for real-time inference
- Communication scheduling for safe swarm operations
- Experimental validation of cyber-physical low-altitude wireless networks
